A country shaped by mountains, rivers and history

Pakistan lies in South Asia, bordered by India, Afghanistan, Iran and China, with the Arabian Sea to the south. It is the fifth most populous country in the world and home to some of the highest mountains on Earth, the mighty Indus River, fertile plains, deserts and a long coastline.

The land was home to the Indus Valley Civilisation, one of the world’s earliest, and later saw Gandhara, Mauryan, Mughal and many other cultures leave their mark. Pakistan was created on 14 August 1947 under the leadership of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah.

  • National language: Urdu — official languages: Urdu & English
  • National sport: Field hockey — most popular: Cricket
  • National animal: Markhor — national flower: Jasmine

Provinces & regions

Punjab

The most populous province. Heartland of agriculture, with Lahore as its cultural capital.

Sindh

Land of the Indus delta, Sufi shrines and Karachi, the country’s biggest city and port.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Home of Pashtun culture, the Khyber Pass, Swat Valley and ancient Gandhara sites.

Balochistan

The largest province by area, with deserts, mountains, rich minerals and a vast coastline.

Gilgit-Baltistan

Home to K2, Nanga Parbat, Hunza and some of the greatest glaciers outside the poles.

Azad Kashmir

Green valleys, rivers and alpine lakes in the foothills of the Himalayas.

Places you must see

Mountains, lakes, valleys and cities — Pakistan has some of the most spectacular scenery in the world.

Gilgit-Baltistan

Hunza Valley

Apricot orchards, snow peaks like Rakaposhi and legendary hospitality. Often called a paradise on earth.

Karakoram

K2 – The Savage Mountain

At 8,611 m, K2 is the second highest mountain in the world and a dream for climbers everywhere.

Hunza

Attabad Lake

A turquoise lake formed by a landslide in 2010, now a top destination for boating and photography.

Gilgit-Baltistan

Fairy Meadows

A lush alpine meadow with a jaw-dropping view of Nanga Parbat, the ninth highest peak on Earth.

Skardu

Deosai Plains

“Land of Giants” — one of the highest plateaus in the world, covered with wildflowers in summer.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Swat Valley

The “Switzerland of the East” with green mountains, rivers, waterfalls and ancient Buddhist heritage.

Baltistan

Skardu

Gateway to K2, with Shangrila Resort, Satpara Lake and cold-desert dunes.

Punjab

Murree

Pakistan’s most popular hill station, close to Islamabad and Rawalpindi, famous for pine forests and snow.

Lahore

Minar-e-Pakistan

A national monument built where the Lahore Resolution was passed in 1940.

Karachi

Mazar-e-Quaid

The white marble mausoleum of Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the founder of Pakistan.

Lahore

Wagah Border Ceremony

A daily flag-lowering ceremony full of energy, parade drills and patriotic chants.

Himalaya

Nanga Parbat

The “Killer Mountain”, rising 8,126 m above the Indus and one of the world’s tallest peaks.

Heritage & historic sites

Pakistan is home to UNESCO World Heritage Sites and some of the finest Mughal architecture ever built.

Lahore · Mughal

Badshahi Mosque

Built in 1673 by Emperor Aurangzeb, it was the largest mosque in the world for about 300 years.

UNESCO

Lahore Fort

A royal citadel with the famous Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors) and grand Mughal gateways.

UNESCO

Shalimar Gardens

Terraced Mughal gardens with fountains and pavilions, built by Shah Jahan in 1641.

UNESCO · 2500 BCE

Mohenjo-daro

A planned city of the Indus Valley Civilisation with drainage and the famous Great Bath.

UNESCO

Rohtas Fort

A massive 16th-century fortress built by Sher Shah Suri near Jhelum.

UNESCO · Gandhara

Taxila

One of the oldest university towns in the world, rich with Buddhist stupas and monasteries.

UNESCO · Thatta

Makli Necropolis

One of the largest graveyards in the world, with half a million tombs and beautiful stonework.

Lahore

Wazir Khan Mosque

Famous for its dazzling tile work (kashi-kari) and calligraphy from the 17th century.

Famous food

Pakistani cuisine is rich, spicy and full of flavour — every city has its own speciality.

Karachi

Biryani

Fragrant basmati rice layered with spiced meat — a favourite at every celebration.

Breakfast of kings

Nihari

Slow-cooked beef or lamb stew, eaten with naan, ideal on a winter morning.

Peshawar

Chapli Kebab

A flat, crispy minced-meat patty full of tomatoes, coriander and spices.

Ramadan favourite

Haleem

A thick stew of lentils, wheat and meat, slow-cooked into a rich comforting dish.

BBQ

Seekh Kebab & BBQ

Skewered minced meat grilled over charcoal — smoky, juicy and unforgettable.

Punjab

Lassi

A cooling yoghurt drink, served sweet or salty, perfect in the summer heat.

King of fruits

Mangoes

Pakistan is one of the world’s top mango producers. Chaunsa and Sindhri are legendary.

Balochistan

Sajji

Whole lamb or chicken, marinated in salt and roasted slowly over an open fire.

Culture & famous things

Colourful traditions, music, sports and craftsmanship that make Pakistan unique.

Folk art

Truck Art

Trucks and buses are decorated with bright paintings, poetry and mirror work — rolling art galleries.

Sport

Cricket

1992 World Cup champions and 2009 T20 champions — cricket is a national passion.

Music

Qawwali

Devotional Sufi music made world-famous by Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan.

Festival

Shandur Polo Festival

Polo played at 3,700 m on the “Roof of the World”, between Gilgit and Chitral teams.

Chitral

Kalash Valleys

A unique community with colourful dress and festivals like Chilam Joshi and Uchau.

Punjab

Khewra Salt Mine

The second largest salt mine in the world, famous for glowing Himalayan pink salt.

National animal

Markhor

A majestic wild goat with spiral horns, found in the northern mountains.

National dress

Shalwar Kameez

The national dress worn by men and women, with beautiful regional embroidery and fabrics.

Festivals

Eid-ul-Fitr & Eid-ul-Adha

The biggest religious festivals, with family gatherings, new clothes and special dishes.

14 August

Independence Day — flags, lights and national songs across the country.

Basant

The spring kite festival of Lahore, celebrating colour and rooftop fun.

23 March

Pakistan Day, with a military parade in Islamabad.

A short timeline

2500 BCE

The Indus Valley Civilisation flourishes at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa.

500 BCE – 500 CE

Gandhara kingdom, Buddhist art and the great university of Taxila.

712 CE

Muhammad bin Qasim arrives in Sindh, introducing Islam to the region.

1526 – 1857

The Mughal era gives Lahore its forts, gardens and mosques.

1930

Allama Iqbal presents the idea of a separate homeland for Muslims in South Asia.

23 March 1940

The Lahore Resolution is passed.

14 August 1947

Pakistan is born, with Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah as its first Governor-General.

1956

The first Constitution is adopted; Pakistan becomes an Islamic Republic.

1973

The current Constitution of Pakistan is adopted.
